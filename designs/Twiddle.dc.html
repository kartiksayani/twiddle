TwiddleA typing practice panel for the moments you're waiting

This board builds out direction 1c Lens with strict mistakes, a header row taken from 1b, and the ⌘-hold commands from 1a. Everything is drawn at 1 pt = 1 px for a 14″ MacBook Pro (1512 × 982 pt) in the dark theme you use day to day, with light variants where the brief asks for them. The panel first appears bottom-centre, 40 pt above the screen edge (your Dock auto-hides), and after that it stays wherever you drag it. Twiddle's accent is violet and is used only for the caret, the next-key hint and the trend line.

02Summoned and resumed

⌃⌥Space brings the panel back with the same text, the same source and today's stats. The caret returns to the start of the word you hadn't finished, so your first keystrokes make a whole word. A violet “Resumed” label replaces the mode until your first keystroke, or for 2 s. The panel never takes focus from the app behind it, so iTerm2 stays the active app.

0 ms: ⌃⌥Space is pressed. The dashed outline marks where the panel will land, which is its last position.
60 ms: fading in and rising 8 pt, with a 180 ms ease-out. There is no scale and no bounce.
180 ms: settled. You had typed “ab”, so the caret is back on the “a”. It blinks until you type.
First keystrokes: the label hands back to “Flow · Words” and the stats carry on from today.
Dismiss
Esc always works: a 120 ms fade with no movement. ⌃⌥Space also toggles the panel closed.
Click away
Clicking any other window hides the panel the same way. The click still goes to that window, and the run is saved.
Idle
After 10 s without a key, the WPM clock stops, the header reads “Paused” and the caret blinks. Nothing else moves, and the next key carries straight on.
Long gaps
The run ends after 5 idle minutes or when you hide the panel. Only active seconds count towards minutes practised.
03Sprint

⌘S switches to Sprint using the last length you chose. Press ⌘S again to cycle 15 → 30 → 60 s. The clock starts on your first key and a 2 pt bar under the header fills as time runs. At 0:00, input pauses for 600 ms so stray keys aren't counted, then the result replaces the text in the same frame. It stays until you choose: ↩ runs again, ⌘F returns to Flow where you left it, and Esc hides.

Ready: you get fresh text, and the stats are blank until the first key.
Running: 18 s left. The clock sits in front of the live stats.
Result, dark: 30 s, 84 wpm, 97.1%, 3 mistakes, slowest pair “br”. The frame stays the same size, so nothing jumps.
Result, light. The keycap for ↩ is filled because it is the default action.
04Content and mode switching

Holding ⌘ for 300 ms shows every command in a capsule above the panel, with the current choices in bold. A quick ⌘ chord never shows the capsule. ⌘1–4 switch the source and ⌘F / ⌘S switch the mode. Each source keeps its own place, so switching away and back loses nothing.

⌘ held. The capsule disappears when you release ⌘, and the chord itself never types anything.
⌘1 Words: lowercase common words with single spaces.
⌘2 Code: real line breaks are shown as ↵ and typed with Return. Leading indentation is skipped for you, so ⇥ is never needed.
⌘2 Code, shell lines: Code mixes Go with the terminal commands you actually type: flags, ~, && and long options.
⌘3 Prose: capitals and punctuation, which means you need Shift and commas.
⌘4 Weak keys: real words heavy in your five weakest keys, with short drills for the symbols (here q, z, [, ], -).
05Finger hints

⌘/ toggles hints, which are off by default. A 116 pt strip opens under the text, and the panel grows upwards so the text stays where your eyes are. The map fills the next key in violet and tints every key that finger owns, using standard QWERTY touch-typing zones. When Shift is needed, the opposite Shift key is outlined. The hints follow the expected key even while a mistake is waiting, so they never grade you.

Off, the default. The dashed area shows the room the strip takes when it opens.
On: next is “o”, so the right ring finger, reaching up from l. The panel is 720 × 252 pt.
On, with a capital: “T” is the left index finger, with the right pinky holding ⇧.
On, light, symbol: “(” is the right ring finger on 9, with the left pinky holding ⇧.
06Progress

⌘P swaps the text for a quick look at today's numbers, the WPM of your last 20 runs and your five weakest keys. Any typing key goes straight back to the text and counts as typed, so a glance costs nothing. ⌘P or Esc also close it.

Dark: 11 minutes, 76 average and 91 best. The weakest keys show average milliseconds per key (sample values).
Light. The filled dot is your best run and the ringed dot is the latest.

▸SwiftUI build notes
Window. NSPanel with .nonactivatingPanel and .fullSizeContentView at level .floating, with canBecomeKey overridden to return true. It takes keystrokes without activating Twiddle, the same way Spotlight does. Collection behaviour: .canJoinAllSpaces, .fullScreenAuxiliary, .transient.
Hide on click-away. Hide on windowDidResignKey, plus a global leftMouseDown monitor while the panel is visible.
Material. NSVisualEffectView using .hudWindow and .behindWindow, state .active, with a tint layer on top (dark: 9% white at 72%, light: 96.5% white at 78%) so text contrast doesn't depend on the wallpaper. Corner radius 16 through maskImage.
Hotkey. Carbon RegisterEventHotKey, or the KeyboardShortcuts package for the recorder. It needs no Accessibility permission, and a failed registration produces the conflict state in 07.
Text. .system(size: 24, design: .monospaced). Draw it as one Canvas pass of glyph runs per line rather than a view per character. AttributedString has no wavy underline, so draw it as a Path under the glyph. Stats use .monospacedDigit().
State. Keep one resume point per source (text seed and caret) in UserDefaults, and runs and key stats in SwiftData. Live stats only count active seconds, which ends after a 10 s gap.
App. LSUIElement set, a MenuBarExtra with .menu style, and SMAppService.mainApp for launch at login. Theme follows NSApp.effectiveAppearance unless it is overridden in Settings.
